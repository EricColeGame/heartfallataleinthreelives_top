## What to Draw: The Subjects Artists Keep Returning To

The most common starting point is the cast. With a dozen-plus named characters and routes that fork aggressively, there is no shortage of faces to practice. Community reports in the itch.io comment thread specifically praise the heart motifs threaded through the in-game art, and that motif is one of the easiest and most recognizable things to carry into your own pieces.

A second reliable well is route divergence. The game's pitch is that every path "diverges wildly" — you might end up playing detective, betraying your first alliance, or unearthing secrets that were better left buried. That structure gives fan artists permission to draw the same character three different ways and call all of them canon-adjacent. Artists who have played multiple routes tend to produce the most interesting compositions because they can put two versions of a character in the same frame.

| Subject Type | Why It Works | Practical Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Cast portraits | 12+ characters with distinct silhouettes | Build a consistent color palette per character before you start |
| Route-divergent scenes | Every path rewrites relationships | Note the route and session number in your caption |

| Music-inspired pieces | 50+ original tracks invite mood boards | Credit the track name if a song drove the composition |

## A Step-by-Step Workflow for Making Heartfall Fan Art

Most artists who post Heartfall pieces follow a similar rough order, and copying that order saves time. The key constraint is research: this game rewards accuracy because so many people have played the same routes and will notice when a character's design drifts.

1. **Pick one route and one moment.** Vague "cast lineup" pieces are harder to finish than a specific scene with a specific emotional beat.
2. **Collect references from the game itself.** Screenshot the cut-in you liked. Do not trace, but do match costume details and color families.
3. **Decide on a spoiler level before you sketch.** If the piece reveals a late-game death or a Watcher reveal, plan the crop and the caption accordingly.
4. **Thumbnail three compositions.** One close-up, one mid-shot with environment, one graphic or symbolic layout. Pick the one that reads fastest at phone size.
5. **Do a value pass before color.** Fan art gets scrolled past in under two seconds; contrast is what stops the thumb.
6. **Write the caption last.** Route name, session number, content warning, credit line, and a link to the game.

| Stage | Time Budget (Typical) | Tool Most Artists Reach For |
|---|---|---|
| Reference gathering | 20–30 min | Screenshots and a private mood board |
| Thumbnails | 15 min | Any brush, low opacity |
| Line or shape pass | 1–2 hours | Clean line brush or lasso fills |
| Value and color | 1–3 hours | Multiply and overlay layers |
| Polish and export | 30 min | 2000px long edge for social |
| Caption and tags | 10 min | Your platform's draft tool |

## Credit, Spoilers, and Content Warnings: Community Etiquette

Heartfall is explicitly an independent, not-for-profit fan project, and the team states plainly that it is unaffiliated with Grian and the 3rd Life SMP cast. That framing shapes how fan art should be handled. The game's own credits spotlight every individual contributor in-game, so the community norm is to extend the same specificity to fan work: name the route, name the scene, and name any artist whose design you referenced.

Content warnings matter here too. The game is rated teen and up and carries warnings for character death, trauma, blood, fantasy violence, animal death, and suicide, plus toggleable flashing and sudden noise. A fan art piece that depicts one of those moments without a warning can land badly on someone who was not expecting it. Tag first, apologize never.

| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Credit Kazehita and the game in every caption | Presenting fan designs as official art |
| Tag the route and session number | Posting late-route reveals untagged |
| Warn for character death and blood | Assuming "it's just a sketch" needs no warning |
| Ask before selling anything | Profiting from a not-for-profit project |
| Link back to the itch.io or Steam page | Reposting someone else's piece without permission |
| Note when a design is your own interpretation | Claiming your headcanon is confirmed lore |

## How Heartfall A Tale in Three Lives Fan Art Grows the Fandom

Every piece posted does quiet marketing work. A single striking portrait on Tumblr or Bluesky reaches people who have never heard of a free visual novel about waking up on a mysterious island, and the game's own page notes that no knowledge of 3rd Life is required to play. That low barrier is exactly why fan art converts so well: a viewer does not need to be a Life Series veteran to appreciate a good composition, and once they are curious, the download is one click away.

The practical takeaway is to make your work easy to trace back. Put the game's name in the caption, not just in a tag pile. Link the official page. Mention the route. A piece that a stranger can follow home is worth three that only existing players will recognize — and the artists who do this consistently are the ones whose galleries become unofficial entry points for new players.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Heartfall A Tale in Three Lives fan art officially allowed?**
The game is itself an independent, not-for-profit fan project that credits all seventy collaborators in-game, so derivative work is very much in the spirit of the thing. Practically speaking, credit the game, credit Kazehita, and ask before you sell anything — the project is explicitly non-commercial.

**Where should I post my Heartfall A Tale in Three Lives fan art?**
Tumblr, Bluesky, and Twitter/X are the liveliest spaces, and the itch.io comment thread gets read by the team. The Steam community hub reaches players who found the game through the store release. Pick two, not five, and keep a personal hub page for the rest.

**How do I avoid spoiling people who have not played yet?**
Tag the route name and session number, put anything past the opening behind a read-more or a content warning, and avoid putting late-game reveals in the first image of a carousel. Community reports show that route names alone are meaningful to players, so they double as accurate spoiler tags.

**Do I need to know 3rd Life before drawing the characters?**
No. The game's own page states that no prior knowledge of 3rd Life is required to play, and the cast is presented through original interpretation rather than as a direct adaptation. Draw what you see in the cut-in art, and note in your caption when you are inventing a design of your own.
